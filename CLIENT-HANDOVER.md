This was the priority throughout, because most of your customers will arrive

## What the site does not say, and why

Some decisions were made deliberately. They are worth knowing about, because
each one protects you.

**No invented numbers.** No years in business, no count of pilgrims served.
Every figure on a website can be checked, and a number that cannot be
defended is a liability. Your trust section works without them.

**No guaranteed visas.** The site says you prepare and submit applications
properly and that the decision rests with the issuing authority. That is the
truth, and it is also what a careful customer wants to hear — agencies
promising guaranteed approval are the ones customers have learned to be wary
of.

**The IATA and airline logos from your office wall are not shown.**
Displaying an accreditation without the certificate behind it creates a legal
exposure rather than a benefit. If you send the certificate or your agency
code, it can be displayed properly — it is worth having.

**No customer reviews have been written.** The section is built and ready but
empty. Reviews will only ever be published in a traveller's own words, with
their permission. Invented reviews are the fastest way for a travel business
to lose its reputation.

## Built to be used by everyone

The site was tested on screens from a small phone to a large desktop monitor,
across every page. It works with a keyboard, it works with a screen reader
for blind visitors, and every photograph carries a written description.

Text contrast was set so the site stays readable on a phone in daylight —
which is where a lot of your customers will be reading it.
